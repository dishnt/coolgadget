How to make a transparent signature PNG

  1. Sign with a dark pen on plain white paper, with no lines or print behind it.
  2. Photograph it in even light, or upload a scan. Avoid casting your own shadow on the page.
  3. Open the photo in the tool above. The signature is cropped automatically.
  4. Raise the threshold until the paper turns fully transparent, then add a little smoothness if edges look rough.
  5. Pick black or navy ink if you want a uniform colour, then download the PNG or copy it.

Use the file in Word, Google Docs, PDF editors, email signatures and e-sign portals. Because the background is transparent, it sits cleanly over any document colour.

Getting a cleaner result

Most rough results come from the photo, not the settings. Uneven light leaves grey patches that survive a low threshold, and a pen that is too light leaves thin strokes that vanish at a high one. If the signature looks broken, lower the threshold a few points. If grey haze remains around it, raise the threshold or retake the photo near a window without casting a shadow.

Smoothness works on the edges only. A value of 2 or 3 suits most phone photos. Higher values round off fine details such as small loops and dots, so use them sparingly on detailed signatures.

Frequently asked questions

Is my signature uploaded to a server?

No. The image is processed with JavaScript on a canvas inside your browser. Nothing is sent to coolgadget.in or anywhere else.

Which format should I use for a digital signature?

PNG with a transparent background is the most widely accepted choice for PDFs, Word documents and email footers. Use JPG only when a portal demands it, since JPG cannot be transparent.

What size should a signature image be?

Most forms ask for a file under 50 to 200 KB and a width of 200 to 600 pixels. The tool exports at full resolution, so resize it afterwards if a portal sets strict limits.

Is a signature image legally binding?

An image of your signature is a convenience, not a certified digital signature. Whether it is accepted depends on the document and the law where you live, so check with the receiving party.
