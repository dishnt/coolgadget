Official Passport Photo Requirements & Guidelines

Most governments follow the ICAO standard for biometric photos, so the core rules are similar worldwide. Details such as dimensions differ by country, so always confirm against the official site of the issuing authority.

Background and lighting

  • Plain white or off-white background with no patterns, shadows or objects (some countries, such as Canada, accept white or light grey).
  • Even, diffuse lighting from the front. Avoid harsh side light, glare on glasses and red-eye.
  • Shoot in daylight facing a window, with the camera at eye level.

Head position and expression

  • Face the camera squarely, with the head upright and not tilted.
  • Neutral expression, mouth closed, both eyes open and clearly visible.
  • Head (chin to crown) usually fills 70% to 80% of the photo height. The US requires 1 to 1 3/8 inches.
  • No hats or head coverings unless worn daily for religious or medical reasons. Glasses are generally not allowed in US photos.

Common specifications

  • US: 2x2 in (51x51 mm), 600x600 to 1200x1200 px for online submission.
  • Schengen / UK: 35x45 mm, which is 413x531 px at 300 DPI.
  • India: 51x51 mm (2x2 in) for the passport.
  • Canada visa: 50x70 mm, with the face 31 to 36 mm tall.
Note: Requirements change. Treat the presets here as a convenient starting point and verify the current rules with your embassy or passport agency before you submit.

Top 5 Reasons Embassy Portals Reject Passport Photo Uploads

  1. Incorrect pixel size. Many portals require exact or minimum dimensions. A photo that is 599x600 can fail. Use a preset to hit the target exactly.
  2. File size over the limit. Upload forms often cap files at 240 KB or even 100 KB. Lower the JPEG quality slider until the size indicator fits.
  3. Shadows on the face or background. Shadows behind the head or across the face are a top cause of rejection. Use soft, frontal light.
  4. Poor DPI or resolution. Prints should be 300 DPI. Screenshots and tiny crops from social media look blurry and get refused.
  5. Wrong framing. A head that is too small, cut off, or off-centre fails automated checks. The guide overlay helps you center your eyes and crown.

Why Local Browser Processing Matters for Privacy

A passport photo is biometric data tied to your identity. Many online tools upload it to a remote server, where you cannot verify how long it is stored or who can access it.

This tool does the work inside your browser using the HTML5 Canvas API. Your image is read into memory on your device, cropped and compressed there, and handed back as a download. There is no upload step, so no server ever receives your photo. You can confirm this by opening your browser’s network tab while using the tool, or by going offline after the page loads.

  • No account and no sign-up.
  • No server copy to leak, sell or forget to delete.
  • Closing the tab clears the image from memory.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will my photo meet digital compliance rules?▾

The tool gives you the exact pixel size and DPI for the selected preset, which covers the technical rules. Lighting, background, expression and head position are up to your original photo, so check them against the official checklist.

Can I print the photo at home?▾

Yes. Print at 100% scale (no “fit to page”) on glossy photo paper. A 2x2 in photo at 600x600 px is 300 DPI. Many pharmacies and photo shops will print the JPG too, usually on a 4x6 in sheet.

What file size limit should I use?▾

It depends on the portal. Common limits are 100 KB, 240 KB or 10 MB. Watch the live size estimate and lower the quality slider until you are under the limit. Quality above 80% is usually still sharp.

Which image formats are accepted?▾

You can upload JPG, PNG or WEBP. The download is always a JPG, since that is the format nearly all embassy and passport portals accept.
